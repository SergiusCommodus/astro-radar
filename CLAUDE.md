# Astro Radar

Rocket launch tracker under the Astro brand. Live at https://astroradar.io (GitHub Pages, deployed from `main`).

## How it is built

- `python3 build.py` assembles `dist/index.html` from `src/`, `data/` and `assets/`, and copies the hero photo files next to it and the planet photos and launch areas (`assets/earth/` to `dist/earth/`) (`earth.webp`, with `earth.jpg` as the fallback; a tiny placeholder is inlined). It also writes `dist/artifact.html`, the same page without a document wrapper and with the photo and the footer's still inlined, since it has no files beside it. Then `launch_pages.py` writes the launch pages, `sitemap.xml`, `robots.txt`, `404.html` and the calendar files, and the end of `build.py` writes `manifest.webmanifest` and `sw.js`.
- The hero photo's calibration and file names are in `assets/earth.json`. `earth_new.jpg` is the master; the web files are encoded from it (WebP 0.85, JPEG 0.84, a 48 by 27 placeholder), so encode them again if the photo changes.
- `src/template.html`: markup, CSS, SVG icon sprite. `src/app.js`: app logic. `src/model.js`: flight path and visibility model (`ORB`). `src/world.js`: the globe card's renderer (WebGL), copied to `dist/world.js`. `src/sw.js`: the service worker template. `launch_pages.py`: the launch pages and calendar files.
- Real Earth (the hero's and the footer's planet; `assets/earth/`, every source, its rights and the research in `assets/earth/SOURCES.md`): full resolution NASA photographs of Earth taken on the International Space Station, public domain (NASA Johnson Space Center, ISS crew photography), credited in the footer. `scripts/make_earth_stills.py` fetches each original (over https), crops it to the band the page shows, resizes it in linear light, crushes the shadows of space to black, fades its top rows into the page color (`--bg`), dithers it by one step (no banding) and saves AVIF and WebP. The hero: ISS074-E-89803, the blue rim over a partly cloudy Pacific west of Chile with the sun's glint, as `hero-wide` (2560 by 1080) and `hero-wide-3840` (3840 by 1620), chosen by `srcset` from 700 px, and `hero-phone` (1200 by 1200); the footer: ISS073-E-246858, a green aurora over a moonlit southern Indian Ocean, `foot-wide` (2560 by 960) and `foot-phone` (1080 by 692). Never invent a place (use NASA's own record). Use only NASA authored photographs with no copyright notice and no third party credit, with no station hardware in the crop, recorded in SOURCES.md first. The hero photo is a `<picture>` (`#heroPst`) filled at once by its inline script, its AVIF preloaded in the head (`VID_HEAD`, the wide one with `imagesrcset`); it sits at the bottom of the hero with its horizon (30 percent down its frame) just under the hero facts (`.hero-film`, `--band`; a `min-height` keeps it filling the band, cropped at the sides); the footer's is asked for when the footer comes within 600 px. Slow motion: both drift (a 4.5 percent zoom and a 1.1 percent pan, 70 s and 90 s each way, `earthDrift`), a CSS transform animation of the picture's own layer, run by the compositor, paused while its block is off screen (`io-off`, `fp-off`), off with reduced motion; no parallax layer, sheen or grain (SOURCES.md says why). No video anywhere (the films of pull request 44 are gone; the sharpest NASA reel, should video return, is listed in SOURCES.md). `?world=off` forces the photo in the globe card, `?world=on` the globe on the low tier.
- Globe card (`#lcard`, `.lcard` in the template; `worldView`, `worldPath`, `drawHero` in `app.js`; `src/world.js`, plain WebGL 1, about 26 KB, loaded by `loadWorld` after the page has painted): the launch (the pad, its modeled path, the rocket, stage separation and the readout) is drawn on a small card, beside the hero copy from 1100 px (`.lc-in` absolute, right of the copy, over the black above the horizon) and right under the hero below that; its caption gives the pad, its place, that the path is modeled and, with a launch area, its satellite and day (`lcCap`). Launch areas (`assets/earth/areas/`, `scripts/make_areas.py`, `areaOf`, `areaFrame`, `#lcZoom`): for 31 sites a 256 km square at 250 m a pixel from NASA GIBS (MODIS Terra or Aqua, or VIIRS NOAA-20, the clearest single day of the last 120, never a blend of days), 1024 by 1024 WebP; `areas.json` is inlined (`#areadata`). On the globe each loop opens on the pad's area with the pad marked, then from 2.6 s to 6.2 s (liftoff is 3.4 s) zooms out into the globe: the image is moved by one CSS matrix from a close up (about 100 km across the card) to the globe's own projection of that patch at the pad (`WD.project`), scale interpolated geometrically, its edge a soft circle; transform and opacity only. Not with reduced motion, in the photo fallback, for a pad more than 70 km from an area's centre, or for a launch with the sun under 6 degrees at the pad (the pictures are daylight; the globe is lit by the real sun). Run the script again now and then. The globe is the world seen from far out (`frameView`: D 2.4 earth radii, looking at the planet's centre from a point 32 degrees south of the pad, north up, the disk larger than the card and lowered by a lens shift, so the pad sits high on the disk and the climb rises off the planet into the black above it; heights exaggerated by `W.liftK`, 0.0045 in the card), lit by the real sun at liftoff (`sunAt`), with day and night maps, relief, clouds, city lights, a thin rim (`thick`, 0.022) and a soft ocean glint. It is drawn once per launch, layout or texture (`WD.ver`, `WV`): no per frame WebGL; the 2k set loads first, the high tier then the 4k set (no 8k tiles: from this distance 4k is sharp). The rocket is drawn on the 2D canvas over it (`#space`) at the display's frame rate (30 on the low tier), only while the card is on screen (`heroOn`, the card's own observer) and not while a phone scrolls (`calm`); the whole flight stays inside the card. A new launch jumps the view (no camera flight). Fallback to the photo with its path (`photoFallback`, `html.photo`, in the card): the low tier, no WebGL, a browser that would render WebGL in software (`failIfMajorPerformanceCaveat`, unless forced on), a lost context, or textures that fail or take over 15 s.
- Quality tiers (`TIERS` in `src/app.js`; `Q()` in `src/world.js` for the globe). High (desktops), medium (every touch device, iOS from the start, computers with four cores or fewer) and low. Every tier gets the hero photo and its drift. The globe card: high draws the globe with the 4k set, relief, cloud shadows and the aurora at up to 2 device pixels; medium the 2k set at up to 2 device pixels (the card is small and drawn once); low the photo in the card. A step down (under 40 frames a second for 3 seconds) moves the card from high to medium, then to low. The photo fallback in the card keeps its own tiers: high (one canvas) and the lite layers for medium and low (layers moved with transform only, a small canvas for stars, lights and sunrise, only the launch drawn each frame).
- Performance: on the lite tiers (phones) nothing is drawn while the page scrolls: a scroll event stops the drawing loop (`calm`, `SCR`) and it resumes 150 ms after the last one, so the hero's canvases and the sky view keep their last frame while the browser scrolls the page on its own; the scroll listener only records the time (passive), nothing follows the scroll, the card's canvases are capped at 2 device pixels (it is small), and no backdrop blur is used on phones. `?fps` also shows the frame rate while scrolling. Profile phones on an emulated 390 by 844 at 3x with the CPU slowed 6x, scrolling with a touch fling (CDP `Input.synthesizeScrollGesture`, scrolled by the compositor as a finger is; `scrollBy` each frame makes the main thread do the scrolling and swamps the difference), and compare the main thread's busy time, idle and during the fling, not only the frame rate (a laptop GPU hides the rest). On a screen faster than 60 Hz, count the frame rate as a 60 Hz phone shows it (the 16.7 ms slots that got a new frame), not raw requestAnimationFrame calls. The planet photos drift on the compositor only while on screen; the card's launch draws only while the card is on screen, Your sky only when it is, and nothing in a hidden tab, so further down the page the main thread is nearly idle; the globe is drawn once per launch, layout or texture (`WD.ver`), never per frame; and slow one time work runs in slices (`JOBS`). Animate CSS with transform and opacity only; no blend modes, filters or backdrop filters over the hero, and no new gradients or canvas fonts set per frame (`font()` sets one only when it changes).
- Simple screens (the simplify block at the end of the style block): the hero is the logo chip, the kicker and status, the launch name, one line with the rocket and pad, the countdown, Watch as the one main button (the reminder takes its place when there is no stream, `updateRemind`), a reminder button and one More menu (`#heroMore`: the provider's page, calendar, Share to Astro, copy link, the second stream; it closes on a choice, a click elsewhere or Escape), and three facts (liftoff, rocket, the view from the ZIP). The data tag shows only when the data is not live. Your sky is the ZIP, one verdict with its reason and where and when to look (`.sky-sum`); everything else is under Details (`#skyDet`; the sky view is sized and drawn only once it is open). A launch row is the logo chip, the short name (`ORB.shortName` in `src/model.js`: the part after the vehicle and pipe, Group and Launch words dropped, parentheses dropped or folded in, the rocket for an unknown payload, at most 24 characters; tested in `tests/shortname.test.mjs`) with the flight tag (`flightTag`: #N, the provider's Nth launch, only when the feed has `n`), the date or countdown (`rowCd`, its own 30 second timer) and the status, in two lines of text at every width (the smoke test checks 375, 390, 430 and 768; on phones the tag sits on the second line, which shows the countdown within a day of liftoff and the date before that). Nerd mode (`#nerdBtn`, `html.nerd`, off by default, kept as `radar.nerd`) adds one compact line (`nerdLine`: vehicle, booster and its flight, pad, orbit, mission type). Above the list one plain sentence (`launchSum`): the next launch the visitor may see from their ZIP, with day, time and direction, or the next launch overall with no ZIP set; a tap opens the full official name, the pad, the view, Watch, Show on radar, the reminder and the links (`S.open` keeps rows open across redraws). No intro or step cards, no kicker labels on section heads. Add something only when it earns its space.
- Layout and fit (the compact pass, rules at the end of the style block): the page is kept short. Panels pad by `--sec-pad`, the hero is at most 720 px tall (100svh less 180, at least 560) with its content unchanged, the launch list shows the next 6 and the providers 6 (those with launches first), each with Show all and Show fewer (`ROWS0`, `PROV0`, `#rowsMore`, `#provMore`; the button sits outside `#rows`, so `renderRows` still rewrites only the rows that changed). The first screen must show the hero, countdown and key facts at 1440 by 900 and the headline, countdown and main Watch button at 390 by 844 (it does down to 375 by 667). Every control is at least 44 px tall; small text links (row links, recent cards, the hero source line, the estimate link, the calendar line, the Space today source links) get an invisible 44 px hit area from an `::after` instead of taller lines. The viewport has `viewport-fit=cover`, so the safe area insets work on an iPhone. No sideways scroll at 375, 390, 430, 768, 1024, 1440 or 1920. The smoke test checks the first screen and the 44 px rule.
- Motion: few and calm, transform and opacity only: the planet photos' slow drift, the launch area zoom in the globe card, the rocket, the countdown digits, the LIVE badge, reveals the first time a block comes into view (`initMotion`) and the Your sky collapsibles opening. Gone on purpose: count ups (`fx` and `countTo` are gone), compass spins and needle hunting, pulsing halos on status dots and tags, idle button glows, the pointer glow on cards and hover lifts. Do not bring them back without a reason. Reduced motion turns the rest off. Keep phone frame rates level: measure a 390 phone at 4x CPU throttle against main after any change.
- The build packs `data/zipdata.json` as base 36 deltas (`pack_zips`) into `dist/zips.json` (about 860 KB, 256 KB gzipped). The page does not carry it: `app.js` fetches it once the page has loaded (`loadZips`, `afterLoad`) and unpacks it (`undelta`). Before it arrives the page starts on the home ZIP saved in this browser (`radar.zipinfo`, written by `setZip`) or on `ZIP0`, the Miami entry the build inlines. `setZip` and sign up wait for the table when someone is faster than the fetch, and say so if it cannot load. A visitor with an older saved ZIP and no `radar.zipinfo` waits for the table once before the first render. `dist/artifact.html` has no files beside it, so it keeps the table inline (`#zipdata`). A page opened from `file://` cannot fetch `zips.json`; serve `dist/` over http.
- Fonts: Barlow 400, 500, 600 and Barlow Condensed 500, 600, latin subset, are in `assets/fonts/` (SIL Open Font License) and copied to `dist/fonts/`. `build.py` writes the `@font-face` rules inline and preloads the four the first screen uses (`FONT_FIRST`), with `font-display: swap` and the size matched fallback faces kept. The fallback faces also carry Barlow's ascent and descent (`ascent-override`, `descent-override`, each Barlow's 100% and 20% divided by the face's `size-adjust`), and the condensed ones are sized on uppercase titles; without that a font arriving after the first paint moved the hero (0.05 at 390 wide with fonts 1.2 s late). The artifact keeps the Google Fonts stylesheet. If a weight is added to the CSS, add the file to `FONTS` in `build.py`.
- Layout shift: lite hero layers hide for two frames after their first size (`box()` in `app.js`), since a layer sized from nothing counts as a shift. Measure with a `layout-shift` observer after any change to the hero markup or the globe card. The hero text the script fills in is also written into the static HTML at build time from the first launch in the inlined feed (`hero_static` in `build.py`: title, status, data note, mission line, the vehicle, pad, orbit and weather facts, the nav countdown name, the Watch button and its source line, and the Share to Astro and Copy link buttons, which the markup keeps hidden for the sample data), so the first paint is final (the first launch is the first one not gone more than 15 minutes, since the page drops a launch 30 minutes after liftoff and is opened 15 minutes after the build on average). A live launch is written live too (the LIVE badge, the Watch button solid and first). Outside the span in which the page would pick that launch in that live state (`hero_window`: until 30 minutes after the last launch listed before it, or from 15 minutes before liftoff when it was not live yet, and after 30 minutes past its liftoff) the head script puts class `dl` on the page, as for a shared link, so main stays hidden until boot instead of changing. From 700 px wide the hero title stays on one line (its size comes from its length, `--n`, written by the build and `renderHero`) and never wraps, so a fallback face much wider than Barlow (Linux has no Arial Narrow to match) cannot make it two lines before the font arrives (that moved the page 0.06 on CI) and the five hero facts are one line each, so another launch has the same height; the Recent launches strip is in the markup from the first paint when the feed has a flight from the last 7 days, with its list's height reserved. The smoke test opens the page right after the build with the mock's launch that left 20 minutes ago, which is outside that span; each `swap` there must match its marker in `src/template.html` and what `renderHero` writes (the build asserts the marker exists). The mission line also has a reserved `min-height` per width (the end of the 600px query block in the template); if you change its font, gap or the pad names get longer, measure the tallest line count again. With no feed the page keeps its sample text and only the reserved height applies. The hero launch is staged from `ORB.staging()`, which reads event times from `EVENTS`, so edit events in `src/model.js`, not in the animation.
- `scripts/fetch_launches.py`: pulls Launch Library 2 and writes `launches.json` (each launch may carry `info`, its official mission page; only https links are kept). GitHub Actions runs it every 30 minutes (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`) and deploys. If the API fails, the script exits with an error and the workflow keeps the last good file.
- The workflow fetches the feed before it builds: `build.py` inlines `dist/launches.json` when it exists (`#feeddata`), so the page opens on live launches with no loading state or layout shift, and still fetches `launches.json` quietly in case the page was cached. A plain local build has no inlined feed: the page shows shimmering placeholders while `launches.json` loads, then live data, or sample data with a note saying why (feed unavailable, offline, or empty).
- Page metadata (title, description, Open Graph and Twitter cards, canonical, icons) is written by `build.py`. `assets/og-image.jpg` (1200 by 630), `assets/apple-touch-icon.png` and `assets/favicon-32.png` were rendered in headless Chrome from `earth.jpg` and `astro-mark.svg`; render them again if either changes. `.gitignore` ignores PNG screenshots everywhere except `assets/`.
- `scripts/fetch_launches.py` keeps every launch in the next 30 days from every provider, suborbital ones included, and the last 7 days of flown ones as `recent` (result success, failure or partial, and the replay link the feed has). One Launch Library request per run covers both (two at most, well inside the 15 an hour limit). Each launch carries a status (go, tbd for to be confirmed, hold, flight for one that has left the pad), a flight profile key from `src/model.js` (NS is New Shepard straight up and down, SUB other suborbital hops, GEN the generic orbital climb), and `vid`/`vsrc`, its official webcast and where it streams; when Launch Library has them, `n` (the provider's launch count with this one, `agency_launch_attempt_count`), `mt` (the mission type) and `bst` (the first stage booster's serial and flight number), all optional so older readers are unaffected (`nerd()`, ported in the Worker). `desc` is the mission description from Launch Library (detailed mode), plain text, hyphens between letters or digits made spaces, placeholders under 25 characters left out, cut to about 400 characters at a sentence or a word (`desc_of`, `descOf` in the Worker: **change the two together**), on upcoming and recent launches; the page shows it, credited to Launch Library 2, in a row's details (`.r-desc`) and on the launch page under The mission. Fan restreams are skipped.
- `data/providers.json`: each provider's name, official site, live page (`live`, `liveSrc`), social page, one line description and the Launch Library names it matches (`match`). The feed script and the page both read it; the build inlines it. Its `notes` list the links left out because they could not be verified (Roscosmos has no verified links at all, CNSA only its website). Check every link with `sh scripts/check_providers.sh` after editing it. Use only official links. Logos: `logo` (a white SVG in `assets/providers/`) with `logoKind` (`word` when it spells the name, which it then replaces; `mark` when it sits beside the name), or `mono` for a monogram badge; `others` gives the same by Launch Library name for providers outside the list (the feed script and the Worker read only `providers[].key` and `match`, so these fields never change the feed). A provider with neither gets a monogram from its name (`mono_of` in `launch_pages.py`, `monoOf` in `app.js`).
- Live feed: `worker/` is a Cloudflare Worker that serves the same feed from Launch Library 2 behind a 5 minute edge cache, with a Durable Object that keeps the upstream calls to 10 an hour and the last good copy (README there has the deploy steps). On HTTP 429 it waits for `Retry-After` (15 minutes without one) and does not count the throttled attempt; with no Launch Library data yet it serves `https://astroradar.io/launches.json` marked `stale` (`X-Feed-Source: fallback`) instead of a 503. `worker/src/feed.js` is a port of `scripts/fetch_launches.py`: **change the two together**; `cd worker && npm test` fails if their output differs. The page reads the Worker URL from the one constant `FEED_URL` in `app.js`, which `build.py` fills from `data/config.json` (`feedUrl`) or the `FEED_URL` variable of the deploy (empty means launches.json only). While the tab is visible `poll()` asks every minute (`fetchFeed`: the Worker, then launches.json, and the file when the Worker serves a stale copy) and `mergeFeed` folds the copy in without a reload: unchanged launches keep their objects, the hero and sky redraw only if the launch on show changed, and `renderRows` rewrites only the rows that changed. `liveOf` is the LIVE NOW rule (within 15 minutes of liftoff or launched in the last 30, never for a time still to be confirmed); the badge, the Watch button first and solid, and the kicker (`Launched N minutes ago`) follow it each second (`liveTick`). `diffEvents` finds the GO, HOLD, SCRUB and LAUNCHED toasts (Launch Library has no scrub status, so a scrub is a go or hold launch within a day moving 15 minutes or more later, or back to a time to be confirmed). The Recent launches strip (`renderRecent`) hides itself when the feed has no `recent`.
- Watch buttons (`watchOpts` in `app.js`, ported to Python in `build.py` for the static hero, so **change the two together**): up to two options per launch. The main button opens the launch's own webcast, else the provider's live page; when the launch has its own webcast and the provider's page is a different link, that page is the second option (the line under the hero buttons, a link under each row). No stream at all says so. Never guess a channel. The button reads "Watch live" only from 15 minutes before liftoff or while live (`liveOf`), "Watch page" before that, with the source ("SpaceX on X", "YouTube", "NASA+") as small text beside it, stacked under it on a phone. The label follows `liveOf` through `liveTick`, so the hero, rows and the featured card change as the time passes.
- Hero copy over the planet: the copy sits in the black space above the horizon, never on the planet. `.hero::before` is a soft dark panel behind the copy only (masked off above the band); `.hero-hz` fades the foot of the hero into the page. No section bands or full width lines: the header (and on phones the bottom tab bar) is opaque on `--bar`, and cards and rows sit on a near solid surface (`--surface`, 90 percent; no backdrop blur, since nothing moves behind them). the page below the hero is deep space (`--bg`) with the sparse starfield, and the footer ends on its own planet horizon. The globe card carries no copy over the globe (`textRects` returns none); the pad tag shows only for PAD OFF MAP. Photo fallback only: a pad inside the photo (`photoPath`: the lower 48 states, southern Canada, Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean) gets its real path; every other pad gets the photo with a path of the right heading and a PAD OFF MAP tag (`offTrack`), so the photo is never presented as that pad's geography.
- Astro tab and Share to Astro: the tab is "Watch with friends on Astro" (what Astro is, the selected launch with Share to Astro and Copy link, Get Astro at https://astrosocial.io, a note that Astro is for ages 18 and over). Share to Astro opens `https://astrosocial.io/app/share?launch=<id>&title=<mission · vehicle>&utc=<ISO UTC>&url=<https://astroradar.io/?launch=<id>>` in a new tab (`astroShare` in `app.js`; the share route itself lives in the astro-app repo). The buttons are on the hero, every row and the Astro tab, and show only for live launch data (`shareOn`), never for the sample manifest. The second button is Copy link, or Share link on a phone that has `navigator.share`. `https://astroradar.io/?launch=<id>` selects that launch (`pickWanted`, `WANT`): the build writes the first launch into the markup, so for such a link the head script puts class `dl` on the page, which hides main until boot (a 2.5 second CSS failsafe) instead of showing a hero that then changes. Set reminder works without an account.
- Where to look (`ORB.visibility` in `src/model.js`, shown by `rateOf`, `ratingText` and `renderRating` in `src/app.js`): a modeled estimate, worked out for every second of the flight. The sun is taken at that second, both for the observer (day, twilight, dark) and for the rocket (above the Earth's shadow at the spot below it, `shadowFromEl`, so a sunlit plume against a darker sky counts). `RANGE` caps how far it can be seen by how it is lit: a daylight trail 200 km, a sunlit plume 950 km, engine flame in the dark 400 km (first stage) and 250 km (upper stage), 150 km in twilight; smaller rockets less (`VSCALE`), except Electron's sunlit plume, which gets the full range (`VSCALE_LIT`: photographed 830 to 1,110 km from Mahia); `docs/VISIBILITY.md` lists the evidence, every change to the model and the sightings it still misses. Under `MINEL` (3 degrees) it is hidden. The rating is the lower of an angle tier (30, 18 and 10 degrees) and a distance tier (150, 250 and 400 km), one step lower for a daylight trail, and a view of under 20 or 45 seconds is held to Low view or Good. The NWS sky cover at launch time can only lower it (25% Great, 50% Good, 75% Low view, 90% likely hidden); with no live forecast clouds are not counted and the page says so (the sample weather is never used for the rating). "Why this rating" lists the sun, distance, direction and weather in plain words, and "How we estimate this" explains it. On the page: the left column has the ZIP, the rating with the compass and four facts; the sky view beside it keeps 16:9 (4:3 on phones) with a maximum height and is shown only when the rocket rises into view (`v.best`). Otherwise it is hidden, so it is not drawn, and the "Not visible from here" card (`renderNv`, `#nvCard`) gives the distance, the direction, the Distance and Direction reasons and the stream. Why this rating (`#whyBox`) is a two column collapsible, open on desktop when the rocket can be seen, closed otherwise and on phones, until the visitor opens or closes it. Distances: every distance shown comes from `distTxt`/`miTxt`/`kmTxt` in `app.js` (whole miles from km x 0.621371 and whole km, rounded half up, thousands separators), including How we estimate this (its `data-km` spans, filled from `ORB.RANGE` and the rating tiers) and the launch pages (`mi` in `launch_pages.py`, the same rule). Never write a distance by hand. The numbers rest on documented sightings: `tests/visibility.test.mjs` lists each source and compares the model with them. When a number changes, change the test and its source with it, and stay cautious: a false Excellent view is worse than a false Low view.
- Launch pages (`launch_pages.py`): `/launch/<id>/` for every launch in the inlined feed (upcoming, in flight, and `recent`), under 20 KB each, sharing `launch.css`, `launch.js` (the visitor's own time, the countdown and the line from the visitor's saved home ZIP, `radar.zipinfo`: distance and direction to the pad against `sky_reach`'s distance for that light) and `mark.svg`: the short name (`short_name`) as the title with the full official name under it, a countdown (`cd_text`, which launch.js keeps current), the Watch buttons right under it (the name, countdown and main button fit the first screen at 390 by 844; the smoke test checks it), then vehicle, pad, location, orbit, mission type, a plain mission summary written only from feed fields (`summary`) and, when the feed has one, Launch Library's own mission description, credited, liftoff in UTC, the pad's zone and the visitor's, status or result, Watch links (`watch_opts`), Share to Astro, Add to calendar, a "Can I see it?" note (`sky_note`: what the light allows, from the sun at the pad and on the rocket about four minutes up, with distances read from `RANGE` in `src/model.js`, and a link to the radar for the per ZIP estimate), metadata, and Event JSON LD for launches not yet flown. `sitemap.xml` lists them; `404.html` sends a page that left the feed to `/?launch=<id>`. On the main page Copy link and the share sheet give the launch page when one exists (`PAGES`, the ids of the inlined feed) and each row has a Launch page link; Share to Astro still sends `?launch=<id>`, which keeps working. Never put anything on these pages that is not in the feed or providers.json.
- ISS pass card (`issCard` in `app.js`, `ORB.issPasses` in `src/model.js`, Nerd mode only, in Space today): the next pass over the visitor's ZIP in the next three days that can be seen (the station sunlit outside the Earth's shadow, a cylinder, while the sun is 6 degrees or more below the observer's horizon, for at least a minute), as one line (day, time, from and to, highest elevation, a brightness hint) plus Details. The elements are the ISS's two line elements from CelesTrak (no key), fetched by `scripts/fetch_launches.py` into the feed's `iss` (`iss_from`; a failed fetch keeps the last good copy, from the output file or the live site, marked stale; `--iss-file` for tests), so the deploy needs no new step; the Worker's feed does not carry them, so the page takes them from the inlined feed. Propagation is satellite.js 5.0.0 (MIT, `assets/vendor/satellite.min.js` with its licence, copied to `dist/vendor/`, loaded only when the card is wanted). Elements over ten days old show nothing. `tests/iss.test.mjs` compares the passes for Miami and New York over 72 hours with Skyfield's (`tests/iss/make_expected.py`, run again only when `tests/iss/tle.txt` is replaced); `tests/test_iss_feed.py` covers the feed.

## Rules of thumb

- Brand: Astro Brand Kit colors (page #04050C, accent #B9A8FF, violet #7C5CFF, cyan #3FD0F0); the page itself now runs on the space look's navy, cool blue and warm tokens, with violet kept to the mark. The mark in `assets/astro-mark.svg` is the official file from astro-app. `build.py` generates both the nav icon (`i-astro` symbol, at the `__MARK__` marker in `src/template.html`) and the favicon from it (the favicon sits on a dark tile, so the white A shows on a light browser tab), so replace only that file. It is the mark in the nav, the footer, the favicon, the apple touch icon and the share image.
- Text stays readable over the brightest part of the world: WCAG AA (4.5 to 1, 3 to 1 for large text) against bright clouds, on a dark surface, never on the planet: the planet is a horizon below the hero copy and below the footer text, never a backdrop to text; body text at least 16 px on phones and 15 px on desktops.
- Visibility results are a modeled estimate, never a promise. Keep that wording honest in the UI: say what is assumed (the flight heading is guessed from the orbit), say when clouds are not counted, and lean cautious.
- Radar has no accounts, sign in or posts. The only things it keeps are in this browser's localStorage: the home ZIP (`radar.zip`, `radar.zipinfo`), set reminders (`radar.rem`) and Nerd mode (`radar.nerd`). Do not add a demo login or invented users, posts or counts; anything that looks live must be live.
- No hyphens in copy shown to the user.
