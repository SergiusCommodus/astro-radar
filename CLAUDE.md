# Astro Radar

Rocket launch tracker under the Astro brand. Live at https://astroradar.io (GitHub Pages, deployed from `main`).

## How it is built

- `python3 build.py` assembles `dist/index.html` from `src/`, `data/` and `assets/`, and copies the hero photo files next to it (`earth.webp`, with `earth.jpg` as the fallback; a tiny placeholder is inlined). It also writes `dist/artifact.html`, the same page without a document wrapper and with the photo inlined, since it has no files beside it. Then `launch_pages.py` writes the launch pages, `sitemap.xml`, `robots.txt`, `404.html` and the calendar files, and the end of `build.py` writes `manifest.webmanifest` and `sw.js`.
- The hero photo's calibration and file names are in `assets/earth.json`. `earth_new.jpg` is the master; the web files are encoded from it (WebP 0.85, JPEG 0.84, a 48 by 27 placeholder), so encode them again if the photo changes.
- `src/template.html`: markup, CSS, SVG icon sprite. `src/app.js`: app logic. `src/model.js`: flight path and visibility model (`ORB`). `src/world.js`: the live world (WebGL), copied to `dist/world.js`. `src/sw.js`: the service worker template. `launch_pages.py`: the launch pages and calendar files.
- Live world (`src/world.js`, plain WebGL 1, no library, about 26 KB, 10 KB gzipped; loaded by `loadWorld` in `app.js` after the page has painted): a 3D Earth fixed behind the whole page. `.hero-art` is one fixed full window layer (`z-index:-1`) holding `#world` (the WebGL canvas) and `#space` (the 2D canvas the launch is drawn on). Textures from `assets/world/` (NASA, see `assets/world/SOURCES.md`; `scripts/build_world_textures.py` makes them): the 2k set loads first on every tier, the high tier then crossfades to the 4k set and loads the 2 by 2 block of 8k tiles (45 degrees each) around the pad (`setDetail`), faded in. Until the first set is in, the page shows its own stars and gradient; then `html.world-on` fades the world in. The Earth is lit by the real sun at the selected launch's UTC time (`sunAt`): day and night maps, relief normals from the height map (plus a fine land detail normal up close on high), an ocean glint on the water mask, soft cloud shadows (high), city lights fading across twilight with a glow read from a blurred mip, clouds on their own shell that drift and change shape through moving noise, and one full screen pass for the blue rim, the warm band along the terminator and a faint aurora (high), over points for stars and a Milky Way pass; colors are tone mapped (ACES fit) in each pass. The camera (`camFor`, `frameView`) sits above a point south of the pad looking at it, with a lens shift (`shift`, added in clip space in every shader and in `project`) that puts the pad where `padSpot` wants it: right of the copy on a desktop, in the gap above the facts at the right on a phone. A new launch flies the camera there (`setView`, 2.2 s, eased, lifted on the way). The scroll (`scrollK`) raises and tilts the planet. Each frame `worldPath` projects the launch's track (`WD.project`, heights exaggerated by `W.lift`) and `drawHero` draws the launch as it did on the photo (pad ring, path, rocket, stage separation, readout); the pad's name goes on only where it covers no text, otherwise the ring alone marks the pad. `?world=off` and `?world=on` override the choice for testing. Fallback to the photo (`photoFallback`, `html.photo`, the reason in the `?fps` overlay): the low tier, no WebGL, a browser that would render WebGL in software (`failIfMajorPerformanceCaveat`, unless forced on), a lost context, textures that fail or take over 15 s, or a step down from medium.
- Hero quality tiers (`TIERS` in `src/app.js`; `Q()` in `src/world.js` for the world). With the world: high (desktops) gets everything at up to 1.5 device pixels; medium (modern phones, iOS from the start) the 2k set only, up to 1.25 device pixels, no cloud shadows, no aurora, 30 frames a second (the launch is drawn at 30 too); low gets the photo. A step down (under 40 frames a second for 3 seconds) moves the world from high to medium, then to the photo. The photo fallback keeps its own tiers: high (one canvas) and the lite hero for medium and low (layers moved with transform only, a small canvas for stars, lights and sunrise, only the launch drawn each frame); it now fills the fixed layer behind the page, so it no longer slides with the scroll.
- Performance: the hero and Your sky stop drawing when off screen or in a hidden tab; the world keeps moving only while the top of the page is near (`worldNear`) and is drawn again only when the scroll moves it further down; with reduced motion it is one still frame, drawn again only when the scroll, the launch or a texture changes (`WD.ver`); and slow one time work runs in slices (`JOBS`). Animate CSS with transform and opacity only; no blend modes, filters or backdrop filters over the hero, and no new gradients or canvas fonts set per frame (`font()` sets one only when it changes).
- Layout and fit (the compact pass, rules at the end of the style block): the page is kept short. Panels pad by `--sec-pad`, the hero is at most 720 px tall (100svh less 180, at least 560) with its content unchanged, the launch list shows the next 6 and the providers 6 (those with launches first), each with Show all and Show fewer (`ROWS0`, `PROV0`, `#rowsMore`, `#provMore`; the button sits outside `#rows`, so `renderRows` still rewrites only the rows that changed). The first screen must show the hero, countdown and key facts at 1440 by 900 and the headline, countdown and main Watch button at 390 by 844 (it does down to 375 by 667). Every control is at least 44 px tall; small text links (row links, recent cards, the hero source line, the estimate link, the calendar line) get an invisible 44 px hit area from an `::after` instead of taller lines. The viewport has `viewport-fit=cover`, so the safe area insets work on an iPhone. No sideways scroll at 375, 390, 430, 768, 1024, 1440 or 1920. The smoke test checks the first screen and the 44 px rule.
- Motion: transform and opacity only. The hero photo moves down a quarter of the scroll on the high tier (`parallax`, `#heroArt`); `html.q-lite` (medium and low tiers) keeps it still and stops the section glows, the compass spins and needle hunting and the selected row's moving border. Numbers count up (`countTo`, the list in `COUNT`, thousands separators kept), the needle sweeps in and hunts gently, the LIVE badge sends out a ring, status dots pulse or breathe, blocks reveal in a stagger the first time they come into view (`initMotion`), and the two collapsibles on Your sky slide and fade open and closed (Web Animations, then `open` changes). Reduced motion turns all of it off. Keep phone frame rates level: measure a 390 phone at 4x CPU throttle against main after any change.
- The build packs `data/zipdata.json` as base 36 deltas (`pack_zips`) into `dist/zips.json` (about 860 KB, 256 KB gzipped). The page does not carry it: `app.js` fetches it once the page has loaded (`loadZips`, `afterLoad`) and unpacks it (`undelta`). Before it arrives the page starts on the home ZIP saved in this browser (`radar.zipinfo`, written by `setZip`) or on `ZIP0`, the Miami entry the build inlines. `setZip` and sign up wait for the table when someone is faster than the fetch, and say so if it cannot load. A visitor with an older saved ZIP and no `radar.zipinfo` waits for the table once before the first render. `dist/artifact.html` has no files beside it, so it keeps the table inline (`#zipdata`). A page opened from `file://` cannot fetch `zips.json`; serve `dist/` over http.
- Fonts: Barlow 400, 500, 600 and Barlow Condensed 500, 600, latin subset, are in `assets/fonts/` (SIL Open Font License) and copied to `dist/fonts/`. `build.py` writes the `@font-face` rules inline and preloads the four the first screen uses (`FONT_FIRST`), with `font-display: swap` and the size matched fallback faces kept. The fallback faces also carry Barlow's ascent and descent (`ascent-override`, `descent-override`, each Barlow's 100% and 20% divided by the face's `size-adjust`), and the condensed ones are sized on uppercase titles; without that a font arriving after the first paint moved the hero (0.05 at 390 wide with fonts 1.2 s late). The artifact keeps the Google Fonts stylesheet. If a weight is added to the CSS, add the file to `FONTS` in `build.py`.
- Layout shift: lite hero layers hide for two frames after their first size (`box()` in `app.js`), since a layer sized from nothing counts as a shift. Measure with a `layout-shift` observer after any change to the hero markup. The hero text the script fills in is also written into the static HTML at build time from the first launch in the inlined feed (`hero_static` in `build.py`: title, status, data note, mission line, the vehicle, pad, orbit and weather facts, the nav countdown name, the Watch button and its source line, and the Share to Astro and Copy link buttons, which the markup keeps hidden for the sample data), so the first paint is final (the first launch is the first one not gone more than 15 minutes, since the page drops a launch 30 minutes after liftoff and is opened 15 minutes after the build on average). A live launch is written live too (the LIVE badge, the Watch button solid and first). Outside the span in which the page would pick that launch in that live state (`hero_window`: until 30 minutes after the last launch listed before it, or from 15 minutes before liftoff when it was not live yet, and after 30 minutes past its liftoff) the head script puts class `dl` on the page, as for a shared link, so main stays hidden until boot instead of changing. From 700 px wide the hero title stays on one line (its size comes from its length, `--n`, written by the build and `renderHero`) and the five hero facts are one line each, so another launch has the same height; the Recent launches strip is in the markup from the first paint when the feed has a flight from the last 7 days, with its list's height reserved. The smoke test opens the page right after the build with the mock's launch that left 20 minutes ago, which is outside that span; each `swap` there must match its marker in `src/template.html` and what `renderHero` writes (the build asserts the marker exists). The mission line also has a reserved `min-height` per width (the end of the 600px query block in the template); if you change its font, gap or the pad names get longer, measure the tallest line count again. With no feed the page keeps its sample text and only the reserved height applies. The hero launch is staged from `ORB.staging()`, which reads event times from `EVENTS`, so edit events in `src/model.js`, not in the animation.
- `scripts/fetch_launches.py`: pulls Launch Library 2 and writes `launches.json` (each launch may carry `info`, its official mission page; only https links are kept). GitHub Actions runs it every 30 minutes (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`) and deploys. If the API fails, the script exits with an error and the workflow keeps the last good file.
- The workflow fetches the feed before it builds: `build.py` inlines `dist/launches.json` when it exists (`#feeddata`), so the page opens on live launches with no loading state or layout shift, and still fetches `launches.json` quietly in case the page was cached. A plain local build has no inlined feed: the page shows shimmering placeholders while `launches.json` loads, then live data, or sample data with a note saying why (feed unavailable, offline, or empty).
- Page metadata (title, description, Open Graph and Twitter cards, canonical, icons) is written by `build.py`. `assets/og-image.jpg` (1200 by 630), `assets/apple-touch-icon.png` and `assets/favicon-32.png` were rendered in headless Chrome from `earth.jpg` and `astro-mark.svg`; render them again if either changes. `.gitignore` ignores PNG screenshots everywhere except `assets/`.
- `scripts/fetch_launches.py` keeps every launch in the next 30 days from every provider, suborbital ones included, and the last 7 days of flown ones as `recent` (result success, failure or partial, and the replay link the feed has). One Launch Library request per run covers both (two at most, well inside the 15 an hour limit). Each launch carries a status (go, tbd for to be confirmed, hold, flight for one that has left the pad), a flight profile key from `src/model.js` (NS is New Shepard straight up and down, SUB other suborbital hops, GEN the generic orbital climb), and `vid`/`vsrc`, its official webcast and where it streams. Fan restreams are skipped.
- `data/providers.json`: each provider's name, official site, live page (`live`, `liveSrc`), social page, one line description and the Launch Library names it matches (`match`). The feed script and the page both read it; the build inlines it. Its `notes` list the links left out because they could not be verified (Roscosmos has no verified links at all, CNSA only its website). Check every link with `sh scripts/check_providers.sh` after editing it. Use only official links. Logos: `logo` (a white SVG in `assets/providers/`) with `logoKind` (`word` when it spells the name, which it then replaces; `mark` when it sits beside the name), or `mono` for a monogram badge; `others` gives the same by Launch Library name for providers outside the list (the feed script and the Worker read only `providers[].key` and `match`, so these fields never change the feed). A provider with neither gets a monogram from its name (`mono_of` in `launch_pages.py`, `monoOf` in `app.js`).
- Live feed: `worker/` is a Cloudflare Worker that serves the same feed from Launch Library 2 behind a 5 minute edge cache, with a Durable Object that keeps the upstream calls to 10 an hour and the last good copy (README there has the deploy steps). On HTTP 429 it waits for `Retry-After` (15 minutes without one) and does not count the throttled attempt; with no Launch Library data yet it serves `https://astroradar.io/launches.json` marked `stale` (`X-Feed-Source: fallback`) instead of a 503. `worker/src/feed.js` is a port of `scripts/fetch_launches.py`: **change the two together**; `cd worker && npm test` fails if their output differs. The page reads the Worker URL from the one constant `FEED_URL` in `app.js`, which `build.py` fills from `data/config.json` (`feedUrl`) or the `FEED_URL` variable of the deploy (empty means launches.json only). While the tab is visible `poll()` asks every minute (`fetchFeed`: the Worker, then launches.json, and the file when the Worker serves a stale copy) and `mergeFeed` folds the copy in without a reload: unchanged launches keep their objects, the hero and sky redraw only if the launch on show changed, and `renderRows` rewrites only the rows that changed. `liveOf` is the LIVE NOW rule (within 15 minutes of liftoff or launched in the last 30, never for a time still to be confirmed); the badge, the Watch button first and solid, and the kicker (`Launched N minutes ago`) follow it each second (`liveTick`). `diffEvents` finds the GO, HOLD, SCRUB and LAUNCHED toasts (Launch Library has no scrub status, so a scrub is a go or hold launch within a day moving 15 minutes or more later, or back to a time to be confirmed). The Recent launches strip (`renderRecent`) hides itself when the feed has no `recent`.
- Watch buttons (`watchOpts` in `app.js`, ported to Python in `build.py` for the static hero, so **change the two together**): up to two options per launch. The main button opens the launch's own webcast, else the provider's live page; when the launch has its own webcast and the provider's page is a different link, that page is the second option (the line under the hero buttons, a link under each row). No stream at all says so. Never guess a channel. The button reads "Watch live" only from 15 minutes before liftoff or while live (`liveOf`), "Watch page" before that, with the source ("SpaceX on X", "YouTube", "NASA+") as small text beside it, stacked under it on a phone. The label follows `liveOf` through `liveTick`, so the hero, rows and the featured card change as the time passes.
- Hero copy over the world: no shade is drawn on the canvas; `.hero::before` is a soft wash behind the copy (no edges), and below the hero `main::before` is one scrim behind the whole page that fades in over 200 px from the hero's foot (`--hero-h`, set by `sizeHero`). No section bands or full width lines: the sticky header and tab bar fade out below themselves, and cards are the only glass (a 7 px backdrop blur on the high tier with the world, a denser glass on the lite tiers). The pad tag and the flight readout avoid the hero copy (`textRects`: title, subtitle, kicker, countdown, buttons, Share row, source line, facts): add any new block in the hero there. Photo fallback only: a pad inside the photo (`photoPath`: the lower 48 states, southern Canada, Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean) gets its real path; every other pad gets the photo with a path of the right heading and a PAD OFF MAP tag (`offTrack`), so the photo is never presented as that pad's geography.
- Astro tab and Share to Astro: the tab is "Watch with friends on Astro" (what Astro is, the selected launch with Share to Astro and Copy link, Get Astro at https://astrosocial.io, a note that Astro is for ages 18 and over). Share to Astro opens `https://astrosocial.io/app/share?launch=<id>&title=<mission · vehicle>&utc=<ISO UTC>&url=<https://astroradar.io/?launch=<id>>` in a new tab (`astroShare` in `app.js`; the share route itself lives in the astro-app repo). The buttons are on the hero, every row and the Astro tab, and show only for live launch data (`shareOn`), never for the sample manifest. The second button is Copy link, or Share link on a phone that has `navigator.share`. `https://astroradar.io/?launch=<id>` selects that launch (`pickWanted`, `WANT`): the build writes the first launch into the markup, so for such a link the head script puts class `dl` on the page, which hides main until boot (a 2.5 second CSS failsafe) instead of showing a hero that then changes. Set reminder works without an account.
- Where to look (`ORB.visibility` in `src/model.js`, shown by `rateOf`, `ratingText` and `renderRating` in `src/app.js`): a modeled estimate, worked out for every second of the flight. The sun is taken at that second, both for the observer (day, twilight, dark) and for the rocket (above the Earth's shadow at the spot below it, `shadowFromEl`, so a sunlit plume against a darker sky counts). `RANGE` caps how far it can be seen by how it is lit: a daylight trail 200 km, a sunlit plume 950 km, engine flame in the dark 400 km (first stage) and 250 km (upper stage), 150 km in twilight; smaller rockets less (`VSCALE`). Under `MINEL` (3 degrees) it is hidden. The rating is the lower of an angle tier (30, 18 and 10 degrees) and a distance tier (150, 250 and 400 km), one step lower for a daylight trail, and a view of under 20 or 45 seconds is held to Low view or Good. The NWS sky cover at launch time can only lower it (25% Great, 50% Good, 75% Low view, 90% likely hidden); with no live forecast clouds are not counted and the page says so (the sample weather is never used for the rating). "Why this rating" lists the sun, distance, direction and weather in plain words, and "How we estimate this" explains it. On the page: the left column has the ZIP, the rating with the compass and four facts; the sky view beside it keeps 16:9 (4:3 on phones) with a maximum height and is shown only when the rocket rises into view (`v.best`). Otherwise it is hidden, so it is not drawn, and the "Not visible from here" card (`renderNv`, `#nvCard`) gives the distance, the direction, the Distance and Direction reasons and the stream. Why this rating (`#whyBox`) is a two column collapsible, open on desktop when the rocket can be seen, closed otherwise and on phones, until the visitor opens or closes it. Distances: every distance shown comes from `distTxt`/`miTxt`/`kmTxt` in `app.js` (whole miles from km x 0.621371 and whole km, rounded half up, thousands separators), including How we estimate this (its `data-km` spans, filled from `ORB.RANGE` and the rating tiers) and the launch pages (`mi` in `launch_pages.py`, the same rule). Never write a distance by hand. The numbers rest on documented sightings: `tests/visibility.test.mjs` lists each source and compares the model with them. When a number changes, change the test and its source with it, and stay cautious: a false Excellent view is worse than a false Low view.
- Launch pages (`launch_pages.py`): `/launch/<id>/` for every launch in the inlined feed (upcoming, in flight, and `recent`), under 20 KB each, sharing `launch.css`, `launch.js` (the visitor's own time) and `mark.svg`: name, vehicle, pad, liftoff in UTC, the pad's zone and the visitor's, status or result, Watch links (`watch_opts`), Share to Astro, Add to calendar, a "Can I see it?" note (`sky_note`: what the light allows, from the sun at the pad and on the rocket about four minutes up, with distances read from `RANGE` in `src/model.js`, and a link to the radar for the per ZIP estimate), metadata, and Event JSON LD for launches not yet flown. `sitemap.xml` lists them; `404.html` sends a page that left the feed to `/?launch=<id>`. On the main page Copy link and the share sheet give the launch page when one exists (`PAGES`, the ids of the inlined feed) and each row has a Launch page link; Share to Astro still sends `?launch=<id>`, which keeps working. Never put anything on these pages that is not in the feed or providers.json.
- Calendar (`docs/ALERTS-DESIGN.md`): `launches.ics` (every upcoming launch, stable UIDs, UTC times, alerts a day and 30 minutes before, folded lines) and `launch/<id>/launch.ics`, at build time; Subscribe to calendar shows under the Launches list and in the footer only when the build had live launches. `icsFor` in `app.js` makes the same event for the hero and rows, so **change `ics_event` and `icsFor` together**. No alert server and no stored data: email and Astro push are designs only.
- Installable and offline: `manifest.webmanifest` and the icons in `assets/` (rendered from the mark: `icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png`, `icon-maskable-512.png`). `sw.js` is versioned by a hash of the page and every file it keeps, so each deploy replaces it and its shell cache. It keeps only the main page, the photo, fonts, icons, logos, manifest and `world.js`, the last good `launches.json`, and the world's textures as the page loads them (cache `radar-world-<hash of the textures>`, so a deploy that leaves them alone keeps them; the page asks for them again once the worker controls it, `swWarm`, so a first visit is kept too; offline with none saved, the world falls back to the saved photo); the page and launches.json go to the network first. The saved launches.json comes back with `X-Astro-Offline`, and the page then says "Offline, showing last update" (`offline()` in `app.js`); never let saved data show as live. `app.js` registers it only over https or on localhost, when the page has the manifest link (not in the artifact).
- Content security policy (a meta tag the build writes, `csp_for`): scripts only from this site or the hashes of the inline scripts in the final page, data only from this site, `api.weather.gov` and the `FEED_URL` origin. A new origin the page fetches must be added to `CONNECT` in `build.py`. Launch pages have a stricter policy with no inline script.
- Space look (the blocks at the end of the style block): a deep navy page (`--bg`, `--navy`) with a cool blue accent (`--cool`, `--accent`) and a restrained warm tone (`--warm`, the horizon and a few highlights). Blocks are soft glass (`--glass` over `--base`, inner glow `--inner`, radius `--r`) with no outline; dividers are hairlines that fade at both ends (`--hair`, `--vhair`); at most one edge of a block is bright (the selected launch's left edge). Borders that set a size are kept transparent, so a restyle never moves anything. Do not bring back outlined boxes or heavy purple.
- Starfield (`#stars`, `SF` and `sfBuild`/`sfDraw`/`sfFrame` in `app.js`): one fixed canvas behind the page. Three depths of stars are each drawn once into a 384 px tile and copied with an offset (drift, and parallax at three rates of the scroll); a few stars twinkle on their own; a pre-drawn streak shoots by every 25 to 70 seconds. It draws only while some of it shows below the hero and the tab is visible: 30 frames a second on the high tier, 20 with about half the stars on medium, and once (still, no parallax) on low and with reduced motion. A tier step down rebuilds it. `.neb` (the nebula) breathes with opacity and transform; `.hero-hz` is the horizon glow and faint rays where the hero meets the page (in the hero, so both hero paths get it). Cards get a light that follows the pointer or a press (`initGlow`, the card's `::before` moved by transform). `html.q-low` keeps all of it still. Measure phone frame rates against main after any change (they were level: 61 to 63 fps at 4x throttle).
- Recent launches strip: a scroller with mandatory snap points, no scrollbar, edges that fade into the page gutter (`--fw`), and arrows on a pointer device of 768 px and up (`rcEdges`): the arrows keep their place from the first paint and turn visible only when the strip overflows, each off at its end.
- Provider logos (`assets/providers/`, sources and licenses in `assets/providers/SOURCES.md`): white, cropped SVGs from Wikimedia Commons (public domain or CC0) shown at 80 percent, full on hover, on the hero (`#heroLogo`, written into the markup by `hero_static`), each row, the Recent cards, the Watch tab provider cards and each launch page; a monogram badge (`.pmono`) when there is none. Each image gets its width and height from its viewBox (every logo about the same area, 3.2 times the height squared, never taller than the slot) so nothing moves when it loads: `logo_html` in `launch_pages.py` and `logoHtml` in `app.js` share the rule, as do `logo_of`/`provLogo` and `mono_of`/`monoOf`, so **change each pair together**. The build copies them to `dist/providers/`, the service worker keeps them, and the artifact carries them as data URIs. Add a logo only from a source that allows it, listed in SOURCES.md, made white with every fill and stroke `#fff`; the footer and launch pages say logos are their owners' trademarks, shown only to identify the provider.
- Weather comes from the National Weather Service in the visitor's browser. If anything live fails, the page falls back to built in sample data and labels it "Sample".

## Testing without network

- Visibility model: `node --test tests/visibility.test.mjs` (physics checks, 39 documented observer cases from 16 launches with sources, and invariants over thousands of observer and time combinations; it prints the comparison table).
- Feed script: `python3 scripts/fetch_launches.py out.json --from-file tests/raw_ll2.json`
- To try the live path locally, build, copy `tests/launches.json` into `dist/`, serve `dist/` over http (`python3 -m http.server 8000 --directory dist`), and open http://127.0.0.1:8000/. `file://` skips the live loading on purpose.
- Mock feed with every provider type and launch sites worldwide (Kodiak to Mahia, Baikonur to Kourou, a fan restream that must be skipped, a pad not picked yet, a launch past 30 days and one in the past):
  1. `python3 tests/make_mock_ll2.py mock.json` (times are relative to now, so run it fresh)
  2. `python3 scripts/fetch_launches.py dist/launches.json --from-file mock.json`
  3. serve `dist/` as above and select each launch: every hero must draw, and the filter bar, provider cards and Watch buttons must cover every provider.
- The real Launch Library sample in `tests/raw_ll2.json` goes through the same two steps (skip step 1). `tests/launches.json` is an older fixture in the page format; do not regenerate or commit changes to it, and never commit `dist/`.
- Worker and page tests: `cd worker && npm test` (parity with the Python feed on `tests/raw_ll2.json`, `tests/raw_ll2_window.json` (a real response), the mock feed and odd records; then the Worker against a fake upstream, cache and clock). `python3 tests/make_mock_ll2.py out.json --now 2026-10-27T12:00:00Z` fixes the clock; without `--now` the mock also has a launch 10 minutes out, one in flight, one that left 20 minutes ago and flown launches with every result. For the page, build with `FEED_URL=http://127.0.0.1:8201/feed`, serve a mock Worker there, and use Playwright's `page.clock` to move a minute at a time.
- Hero tiers: add `?fps` to the URL for the diagnostics overlay (frame rate, frame and drawing time, canvas size, device pixel ratio, tier), and `?q=high`, `?q=medium` or `?q=low` to force a tier, `?world=off` or `?world=on` to force the photo or the world (the overlay shows the world's level, canvas, pixel ratio and detail, or why it fell back). Headless Chrome on a machine with a GPU renders the world on the GPU; `--use-angle=swiftshader --enable-unsafe-swiftshader` renders it in software as CI does. WebKit on Windows composites in software, so it shows the lite tiers slower than an iPhone would; judge them on a real iPhone with `?fps`.
- Launch pages, calendar files and provider logos: after a build with a feed, `python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -p "test_*.py"` (`tests/test_logos.py` fails if a provider in providers.json or in any feed sample has neither a logo nor a monogram, if a logo file is unsafe, not white, not in SOURCES.md or not kept by the service worker, or if the page's monogram rule differs from the build's).

## Rules of thumb

- Brand: Astro Brand Kit colors (page #04050C, accent #B9A8FF, violet #7C5CFF, cyan #3FD0F0); the page itself now runs on the space look's navy, cool blue and warm tokens, with violet kept to the mark. The mark in `assets/astro-mark.svg` is the official file from astro-app. `build.py` generates both the nav icon (`i-astro` symbol, at the `__MARK__` marker in `src/template.html`) and the favicon from it (the favicon sits on a dark tile, so the white A shows on a light browser tab), so replace only that file. It is the mark in the nav, the footer, the favicon, the apple touch icon and the share image.
- Visibility results are a modeled estimate, never a promise. Keep that wording honest in the UI: say what is assumed (the flight heading is guessed from the orbit), say when clouds are not counted, and lean cautious.
- Radar has no accounts, sign in or posts. The only things it keeps are in this browser's localStorage: the home ZIP (`radar.zip`, `radar.zipinfo`) and set reminders (`radar.rem`). Do not add a demo login or invented users, posts or counts; anything that looks live must be live.
- No hyphens in copy shown to the user.
