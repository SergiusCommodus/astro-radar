# Astro Radar

Rocket launch tracker under the Astro brand. Live at https://astroradar.io (GitHub Pages, deployed from `main`).

## How it is built

- `python3 build.py` assembles `dist/index.html` from `src/`, `data/` and `assets/`, and copies the hero photo files next to it and the footer's planet loop (`assets/loop/` to `dist/loop/`) (`earth.webp`, with `earth.jpg` as the fallback; a tiny placeholder is inlined). It also writes `dist/artifact.html`, the same page without a document wrapper and with the photo inlined, since it has no files beside it. Then `launch_pages.py` writes the launch pages, `sitemap.xml`, `robots.txt`, `404.html` and the calendar files, and the end of `build.py` writes `manifest.webmanifest` and `sw.js`.
- The hero photo's calibration and file names are in `assets/earth.json`. `earth_new.jpg` is the master; the web files are encoded from it (WebP 0.85, JPEG 0.84, a 48 by 27 placeholder), so encode them again if the photo changes.
- `src/template.html`: markup, CSS, SVG icon sprite. `src/app.js`: app logic. `src/model.js`: flight path and visibility model (`ORB`). `src/world.js`: the live world (WebGL), copied to `dist/world.js`. `src/sw.js`: the service worker template. `launch_pages.py`: the launch pages and calendar files.
- Live world (`src/world.js`, plain WebGL 1, no library, about 27 KB; loaded by `loadWorld` in `app.js` after the page has painted): a 3D Earth in the hero only, framed as a low orbit limb view, on desktops (the high tier; the medium and low tiers get the hero loop, see Hero loop). `.hero-art` is the hero's own layer (absolute, as tall as the hero, opaque) holding `#world` (the WebGL canvas) and `#space` (the 2D canvas the launch is drawn on); nothing of the world is fixed behind the page. The hero keeps a band below its copy (`--band`, the hero's padding below the facts): the top of the horizon sits just under the hero facts, so the copy is always on black space and the planet only fills the band (`padSpot` gives the pad's x, the horizon's y and the pad's y as fractions of the hero; `frameView` in `world.js` works out the camera: D earth radii out (1.85 on desktops, 1.6 on phones; lower than 1.85 the 4k night and cloud maps are magnified two or three times and city lights turn blocky), a fixed horizontal field of view (`hf`, 56 degrees on desktops; the vertical one follows the canvas's shape, so the planet spans the full width from 768 to 1920 and beyond), looking north over the pad, the pitch putting the horizon and the pad either side of the axis, the distance to the pad chosen so their gap is the one asked for; the top of the horizon is always in the middle of the picture (centred, no empty side), the camera turns sideways until the pad sits at the x asked for (right of the copy), and a lens shift (`shift`, added in clip space in every shader and in `project`) moves the horizon up or down to its place). The world is drawn only while at least a third of the hero is on screen (`heroOn`, its own observer) and never follows the scroll (`scrollK` is 0). Textures from `assets/world/` (NASA, see `assets/world/SOURCES.md`; `scripts/build_world_textures.py` makes them; the cloud map is one day of satellite data, so it blends across the date line, `wrap_seam`): the 2k set loads first on every tier, the high tier then crossfades to the 4k set and loads the 2 by 2 block of 8k tiles (45 degrees each) around the pad (`setDetail`), faded in. Until the first set is in, the hero is black; then `html.world-on` fades the world in. The Earth is lit by the real sun at the selected launch's UTC time (`sunAt`): day and night maps, relief normals from the height map (plus a fine land detail normal up close on high), an ocean glint on the water mask, soft cloud shadows (high), city lights fading across twilight with a glow read from a blurred mip, clouds on their own shell that drift and change shape through moving noise, and one full screen pass for the blue rim, the haze along the limb (kept thin: the limb view looks through a lot of it), the warm band along the terminator and a faint aurora (high), over points for stars and a Milky Way pass; colors are tone mapped (ACES fit) in each pass. A new launch flies the camera there (`setView`, 2.2 s, eased, lifted on the way), and a slow drift turns the view a little. Each frame `worldPath` projects the launch's track (`WD.project`, heights exaggerated by `W.lift`) and `drawHero` draws the launch as it did on the photo (pad ring, path, rocket, stage separation, readout), rising from the planet into the black above; the pad's name goes on only where it covers no text, otherwise the ring alone marks the pad. `?world=off` and `?world=on` override the choice for testing. Fallback to the photo (`photoFallback`, `html.photo`, the reason in the `?fps` overlay): the low tier, no WebGL, a browser that would render WebGL in software (`failIfMajorPerformanceCaveat`, unless forced on), a lost context, or textures that fail or take over 15 s. The photo fills the hero as before (it looks down on the planet, so it sits under the copy's dark panel; only the world gives the limb layout).
- Hero loop (`VID`, `heroVidInit`, `heroVidSync` and `videoPath` in `app.js`, `.hero-vid` in the template): the medium and low tiers (every phone and tablet, and low power computers) get the hero's planet as a short seamless loop rendered offline from this same world instead of live WebGL, so no WebGL runs there at all and the browser decodes the video off the main thread: `loop/hero-phone.*` (1080 by 1350) under 700 px wide and `loop/hero-wide.*` (1920 by 1080) above, WebM (VP9) where VP9 plays and the browser is not Safari, else MP4 (H.264 high), rendered supersampled with a fixed dither against banding and encoded in two passes at the bitrate that fills their caps (1.6 MB phone, 2.4 MB wide; `node scripts/render_loops.cjs hero-phone hero-wide`, on a machine with a GPU), with a sharp WebP poster (quality 88) shown at once. The video sits at the bottom of the hero with its horizon (30 percent down its frame) just under the hero facts, like the world's; its top rows fade into the page color (rendered that way), so the hero is transparent there and the page's stars show above it. It loads after the page has loaded, muted, looped and inline, plays only while the hero is on screen and the tab is visible, and is replaced by its poster with reduced motion, Save Data or 2G, or when the browser refuses to play it (iOS Low Power Mode). The loop is a generic limb, not the pad's geography: `videoPath` puts the pad at a fixed spot on its surface (`padSpot`) and lays the track out from it by its real heading (east to the right, north into the distance, up by altitude); the launch is drawn over it as over the world. `?world=on` forces the live world, `?world=off` the photo, any other value the page's own choice.
- Hero quality tiers (`TIERS` in `src/app.js`; `Q()` in `src/world.js` for the world). High (desktops) gets the live world at up to 1.5 device pixels; medium (every touch device, iOS from the start, computers with four cores or fewer) and low get the hero loop, with the launch drawn over it at 24 frames a second. Forced on (`?world=on`), the world on medium is the 2k set only, up to 1.25 device pixels, no cloud shadows, no aurora, 24 frames a second. A step down (under 40 frames a second for 3 seconds) moves the world from high to medium, and from medium to one still frame of the limb (`WSTILL`, `wStill()`: the renderer draws the current view once, and again only when the launch, the layout or a texture changes; the launch keeps moving over it). The photo fallback keeps its own tiers: high (one canvas) and the lite hero for medium and low (layers moved with transform only, a small canvas for stars, lights and sunrise, only the launch drawn each frame), inside the hero's own layer.
- Performance: on the lite tiers (phones) nothing is drawn while the page scrolls: a scroll event stops the drawing loop (`calm`, `SCR`) and it resumes 150 ms after the last one, so the hero's canvases and the sky view keep their last frame while the browser scrolls the page on its own; the scroll listener only records the time (passive), nothing follows the scroll, canvases are capped at 1.25 device pixels and ambient motion at 24 frames a second, and no backdrop blur is used on phones. `?fps` also shows the frame rate while scrolling. Profile phones on an emulated 390 by 844 at 3x with the CPU slowed 6x, scrolling with a touch fling (CDP `Input.synthesizeScrollGesture`, scrolled by the compositor as a finger is; `scrollBy` each frame makes the main thread do the scrolling and swamps the difference), and compare the main thread's busy time, idle and during the fling, not only the frame rate (a laptop GPU hides the rest). On a screen faster than 60 Hz, count the frame rate as a 60 Hz phone shows it (the 16.7 ms slots that got a new frame), not raw requestAnimationFrame calls. The hero (world and launch) stops drawing once less than a third of it is on screen, Your sky when it is off screen, and both in a hidden tab, so further down the page the main thread is nearly idle; phones run no WebGL at all (the hero loop); with reduced motion the world is one still frame, drawn again only when the launch, the layout or a texture changes (`WD.ver`); and slow one time work runs in slices (`JOBS`). Animate CSS with transform and opacity only; no blend modes, filters or backdrop filters over the hero, and no new gradients or canvas fonts set per frame (`font()` sets one only when it changes).
- Simple screens (the simplify block at the end of the style block): the hero is the logo chip, the kicker and status, the launch name, one line with the rocket and pad, the countdown, Watch as the one main button (the reminder takes its place when there is no stream, `updateRemind`), a reminder button and one More menu (`#heroMore`: the provider's page, calendar, Share to Astro, copy link, the second stream; it closes on a choice, a click elsewhere or Escape), and three facts (liftoff, rocket, the view from the ZIP). The data tag shows only when the data is not live. Your sky is the ZIP, one verdict with its reason and where and when to look (`.sky-sum`); everything else is under Details (`#skyDet`; the sky view is sized and drawn only once it is open). A launch row is the logo chip, the short name (`ORB.shortName` in `src/model.js`: the part after the vehicle and pipe, Group and Launch words dropped, parentheses dropped or folded in, the rocket for an unknown payload, at most 24 characters; tested in `tests/shortname.test.mjs`) with the flight tag (`flightTag`: #N, the provider's Nth launch, only when the feed has `n`), the date or countdown (`rowCd`, its own 30 second timer) and the status, in two lines of text at every width (the smoke test checks 375, 390, 430 and 768; on phones the tag sits on the second line, which shows the countdown within a day of liftoff and the date before that). Nerd mode (`#nerdBtn`, `html.nerd`, off by default, kept as `radar.nerd`) adds one compact line (`nerdLine`: vehicle, booster and its flight, pad, orbit, mission type). Above the list one plain sentence (`launchSum`): the next launch the visitor may see from their ZIP, with day, time and direction, or the next launch overall with no ZIP set; a tap opens the full official name, the pad, the view, Watch, Show on radar, the reminder and the links (`S.open` keeps rows open across redraws). No intro or step cards, no kicker labels on section heads. Add something only when it earns its space.
- Layout and fit (the compact pass, rules at the end of the style block): the page is kept short. Panels pad by `--sec-pad`, the hero is at most 720 px tall (100svh less 180, at least 560) with its content unchanged, the launch list shows the next 6 and the providers 6 (those with launches first), each with Show all and Show fewer (`ROWS0`, `PROV0`, `#rowsMore`, `#provMore`; the button sits outside `#rows`, so `renderRows` still rewrites only the rows that changed). The first screen must show the hero, countdown and key facts at 1440 by 900 and the headline, countdown and main Watch button at 390 by 844 (it does down to 375 by 667). Every control is at least 44 px tall; small text links (row links, recent cards, the hero source line, the estimate link, the calendar line, the Space today source links) get an invisible 44 px hit area from an `::after` instead of taller lines. The viewport has `viewport-fit=cover`, so the safe area insets work on an iPhone. No sideways scroll at 375, 390, 430, 768, 1024, 1440 or 1920. The smoke test checks the first screen and the 44 px rule.
- Motion: few and calm, transform and opacity only: the planet, the rocket, the countdown digits, the LIVE badge, reveals the first time a block comes into view (`initMotion`) and the Your sky collapsibles opening. Gone on purpose: count ups (`fx` does nothing), compass spins and needle hunting, pulsing halos on status dots and tags, idle button glows, the pointer glow on cards and hover lifts. Do not bring them back without a reason. Reduced motion turns the rest off. Keep phone frame rates level: measure a 390 phone at 4x CPU throttle against main after any change.
- The build packs `data/zipdata.json` as base 36 deltas (`pack_zips`) into `dist/zips.json` (about 860 KB, 256 KB gzipped). The page does not carry it: `app.js` fetches it once the page has loaded (`loadZips`, `afterLoad`) and unpacks it (`undelta`). Before it arrives the page starts on the home ZIP saved in this browser (`radar.zipinfo`, written by `setZip`) or on `ZIP0`, the Miami entry the build inlines. `setZip` and sign up wait for the table when someone is faster than the fetch, and say so if it cannot load. A visitor with an older saved ZIP and no `radar.zipinfo` waits for the table once before the first render. `dist/artifact.html` has no files beside it, so it keeps the table inline (`#zipdata`). A page opened from `file://` cannot fetch `zips.json`; serve `dist/` over http.
- Fonts: Barlow 400, 500, 600 and Barlow Condensed 500, 600, latin subset, are in `assets/fonts/` (SIL Open Font License) and copied to `dist/fonts/`. `build.py` writes the `@font-face` rules inline and preloads the four the first screen uses (`FONT_FIRST`), with `font-display: swap` and the size matched fallback faces kept. The fallback faces also carry Barlow's ascent and descent (`ascent-override`, `descent-override`, each Barlow's 100% and 20% divided by the face's `size-adjust`), and the condensed ones are sized on uppercase titles; without that a font arriving after the first paint moved the hero (0.05 at 390 wide with fonts 1.2 s late). The artifact keeps the Google Fonts stylesheet. If a weight is added to the CSS, add the file to `FONTS` in `build.py`.
- Layout shift: lite hero layers hide for two frames after their first size (`box()` in `app.js`), since a layer sized from nothing counts as a shift. Measure with a `layout-shift` observer after any change to the hero markup. The hero text the script fills in is also written into the static HTML at build time from the first launch in the inlined feed (`hero_static` in `build.py`: title, status, data note, mission line, the vehicle, pad, orbit and weather facts, the nav countdown name, the Watch button and its source line, and the Share to Astro and Copy link buttons, which the markup keeps hidden for the sample data), so the first paint is final (the first launch is the first one not gone more than 15 minutes, since the page drops a launch 30 minutes after liftoff and is opened 15 minutes after the build on average). A live launch is written live too (the LIVE badge, the Watch button solid and first). Outside the span in which the page would pick that launch in that live state (`hero_window`: until 30 minutes after the last launch listed before it, or from 15 minutes before liftoff when it was not live yet, and after 30 minutes past its liftoff) the head script puts class `dl` on the page, as for a shared link, so main stays hidden until boot instead of changing. From 700 px wide the hero title stays on one line (its size comes from its length, `--n`, written by the build and `renderHero`) and never wraps, so a fallback face much wider than Barlow (Linux has no Arial Narrow to match) cannot make it two lines before the font arrives (that moved the page 0.06 on CI) and the five hero facts are one line each, so another launch has the same height; the Recent launches strip is in the markup from the first paint when the feed has a flight from the last 7 days, with its list's height reserved. The smoke test opens the page right after the build with the mock's launch that left 20 minutes ago, which is outside that span; each `swap` there must match its marker in `src/template.html` and what `renderHero` writes (the build asserts the marker exists). The mission line also has a reserved `min-height` per width (the end of the 600px query block in the template); if you change its font, gap or the pad names get longer, measure the tallest line count again. With no feed the page keeps its sample text and only the reserved height applies. The hero launch is staged from `ORB.staging()`, which reads event times from `EVENTS`, so edit events in `src/model.js`, not in the animation.
- `scripts/fetch_launches.py`: pulls Launch Library 2 and writes `launches.json` (each launch may carry `info`, its official mission page; only https links are kept). GitHub Actions runs it every 30 minutes (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`) and deploys. If the API fails, the script exits with an error and the workflow keeps the last good file.
- The workflow fetches the feed before it builds: `build.py` inlines `dist/launches.json` when it exists (`#feeddata`), so the page opens on live launches with no loading state or layout shift, and still fetches `launches.json` quietly in case the page was cached. A plain local build has no inlined feed: the page shows shimmering placeholders while `launches.json` loads, then live data, or sample data with a note saying why (feed unavailable, offline, or empty).
- Page metadata (title, description, Open Graph and Twitter cards, canonical, icons) is written by `build.py`. `assets/og-image.jpg` (1200 by 630), `assets/apple-touch-icon.png` and `assets/favicon-32.png` were rendered in headless Chrome from `earth.jpg` and `astro-mark.svg`; render them again if either changes. `.gitignore` ignores PNG screenshots everywhere except `assets/`.
- `scripts/fetch_launches.py` keeps every launch in the next 30 days from every provider, suborbital ones included, and the last 7 days of flown ones as `recent` (result success, failure or partial, and the replay link the feed has). One Launch Library request per run covers both (two at most, well inside the 15 an hour limit). Each launch carries a status (go, tbd for to be confirmed, hold, flight for one that has left the pad), a flight profile key from `src/model.js` (NS is New Shepard straight up and down, SUB other suborbital hops, GEN the generic orbital climb), and `vid`/`vsrc`, its official webcast and where it streams; when Launch Library has them, `n` (the provider's launch count with this one, `agency_launch_attempt_count`), `mt` (the mission type) and `bst` (the first stage booster's serial and flight number), all optional so older readers are unaffected (`nerd()`, ported in the Worker). Fan restreams are skipped.
- `data/providers.json`: each provider's name, official site, live page (`live`, `liveSrc`), social page, one line description and the Launch Library names it matches (`match`). The feed script and the page both read it; the build inlines it. Its `notes` list the links left out because they could not be verified (Roscosmos has no verified links at all, CNSA only its website). Check every link with `sh scripts/check_providers.sh` after editing it. Use only official links. Logos: `logo` (a white SVG in `assets/providers/`) with `logoKind` (`word` when it spells the name, which it then replaces; `mark` when it sits beside the name), or `mono` for a monogram badge; `others` gives the same by Launch Library name for providers outside the list (the feed script and the Worker read only `providers[].key` and `match`, so these fields never change the feed). A provider with neither gets a monogram from its name (`mono_of` in `launch_pages.py`, `monoOf` in `app.js`).
- Live feed: `worker/` is a Cloudflare Worker that serves the same feed from Launch Library 2 behind a 5 minute edge cache, with a Durable Object that keeps the upstream calls to 10 an hour and the last good copy (README there has the deploy steps). On HTTP 429 it waits for `Retry-After` (15 minutes without one) and does not count the throttled attempt; with no Launch Library data yet it serves `https://astroradar.io/launches.json` marked `stale` (`X-Feed-Source: fallback`) instead of a 503. `worker/src/feed.js` is a port of `scripts/fetch_launches.py`: **change the two together**; `cd worker && npm test` fails if their output differs. The page reads the Worker URL from the one constant `FEED_URL` in `app.js`, which `build.py` fills from `data/config.json` (`feedUrl`) or the `FEED_URL` variable of the deploy (empty means launches.json only). While the tab is visible `poll()` asks every minute (`fetchFeed`: the Worker, then launches.json, and the file when the Worker serves a stale copy) and `mergeFeed` folds the copy in without a reload: unchanged launches keep their objects, the hero and sky redraw only if the launch on show changed, and `renderRows` rewrites only the rows that changed. `liveOf` is the LIVE NOW rule (within 15 minutes of liftoff or launched in the last 30, never for a time still to be confirmed); the badge, the Watch button first and solid, and the kicker (`Launched N minutes ago`) follow it each second (`liveTick`). `diffEvents` finds the GO, HOLD, SCRUB and LAUNCHED toasts (Launch Library has no scrub status, so a scrub is a go or hold launch within a day moving 15 minutes or more later, or back to a time to be confirmed). The Recent launches strip (`renderRecent`) hides itself when the feed has no `recent`.
- Watch buttons (`watchOpts` in `app.js`, ported to Python in `build.py` for the static hero, so **change the two together**): up to two options per launch. The main button opens the launch's own webcast, else the provider's live page; when the launch has its own webcast and the provider's page is a different link, that page is the second option (the line under the hero buttons, a link under each row). No stream at all says so. Never guess a channel. The button reads "Watch live" only from 15 minutes before liftoff or while live (`liveOf`), "Watch page" before that, with the source ("SpaceX on X", "YouTube", "NASA+") as small text beside it, stacked under it on a phone. The label follows `liveOf` through `liveTick`, so the hero, rows and the featured card change as the time passes.
- Hero copy over the world: the copy sits in the black space above the horizon, never on the planet. `.hero::before` is a soft dark panel behind the copy only (masked off above the band); `.hero-hz` fades the foot of the hero into the page. No section bands or full width lines: the header (and on phones the bottom tab bar) is opaque on `--bar`, and cards and rows sit on a near solid surface (`--surface`, 90 percent; no backdrop blur, since nothing moves behind them). `main::before` (the old page scrim) is off: the page below the hero is deep space (`--bg`) with the sparse starfield, and the footer ends on its own planet horizon. The pad tag and the flight readout avoid the hero copy (`textRects`: title, subtitle, kicker, countdown, buttons, Share row, source line, facts): add any new block in the hero there. Photo fallback only: a pad inside the photo (`photoPath`: the lower 48 states, southern Canada, Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean) gets its real path; every other pad gets the photo with a path of the right heading and a PAD OFF MAP tag (`offTrack`), so the photo is never presented as that pad's geography.
- Astro tab and Share to Astro: the tab is "Watch with friends on Astro" (what Astro is, the selected launch with Share to Astro and Copy link, Get Astro at https://astrosocial.io, a note that Astro is for ages 18 and over). Share to Astro opens `https://astrosocial.io/app/share?launch=<id>&title=<mission · vehicle>&utc=<ISO UTC>&url=<https://astroradar.io/?launch=<id>>` in a new tab (`astroShare` in `app.js`; the share route itself lives in the astro-app repo). The buttons are on the hero, every row and the Astro tab, and show only for live launch data (`shareOn`), never for the sample manifest. The second button is Copy link, or Share link on a phone that has `navigator.share`. `https://astroradar.io/?launch=<id>` selects that launch (`pickWanted`, `WANT`): the build writes the first launch into the markup, so for such a link the head script puts class `dl` on the page, which hides main until boot (a 2.5 second CSS failsafe) instead of showing a hero that then changes. Set reminder works without an account.
- Where to look (`ORB.visibility` in `src/model.js`, shown by `rateOf`, `ratingText` and `renderRating` in `src/app.js`): a modeled estimate, worked out for every second of the flight. The sun is taken at that second, both for the observer (day, twilight, dark) and for the rocket (above the Earth's shadow at the spot below it, `shadowFromEl`, so a sunlit plume against a darker sky counts). `RANGE` caps how far it can be seen by how it is lit: a daylight trail 200 km, a sunlit plume 950 km, engine flame in the dark 400 km (first stage) and 250 km (upper stage), 150 km in twilight; smaller rockets less (`VSCALE`). Under `MINEL` (3 degrees) it is hidden. The rating is the lower of an angle tier (30, 18 and 10 degrees) and a distance tier (150, 250 and 400 km), one step lower for a daylight trail, and a view of under 20 or 45 seconds is held to Low view or Good. The NWS sky cover at launch time can only lower it (25% Great, 50% Good, 75% Low view, 90% likely hidden); with no live forecast clouds are not counted and the page says so (the sample weather is never used for the rating). "Why this rating" lists the sun, distance, direction and weather in plain words, and "How we estimate this" explains it. On the page: the left column has the ZIP, the rating with the compass and four facts; the sky view beside it keeps 16:9 (4:3 on phones) with a maximum height and is shown only when the rocket rises into view (`v.best`). Otherwise it is hidden, so it is not drawn, and the "Not visible from here" card (`renderNv`, `#nvCard`) gives the distance, the direction, the Distance and Direction reasons and the stream. Why this rating (`#whyBox`) is a two column collapsible, open on desktop when the rocket can be seen, closed otherwise and on phones, until the visitor opens or closes it. Distances: every distance shown comes from `distTxt`/`miTxt`/`kmTxt` in `app.js` (whole miles from km x 0.621371 and whole km, rounded half up, thousands separators), including How we estimate this (its `data-km` spans, filled from `ORB.RANGE` and the rating tiers) and the launch pages (`mi` in `launch_pages.py`, the same rule). Never write a distance by hand. The numbers rest on documented sightings: `tests/visibility.test.mjs` lists each source and compares the model with them. When a number changes, change the test and its source with it, and stay cautious: a false Excellent view is worse than a false Low view.
- Launch pages (`launch_pages.py`): `/launch/<id>/` for every launch in the inlined feed (upcoming, in flight, and `recent`), under 20 KB each, sharing `launch.css`, `launch.js` (the visitor's own time) and `mark.svg`: name, vehicle, pad, liftoff in UTC, the pad's zone and the visitor's, status or result, Watch links (`watch_opts`), Share to Astro, Add to calendar, a "Can I see it?" note (`sky_note`: what the light allows, from the sun at the pad and on the rocket about four minutes up, with distances read from `RANGE` in `src/model.js`, and a link to the radar for the per ZIP estimate), metadata, and Event JSON LD for launches not yet flown. `sitemap.xml` lists them; `404.html` sends a page that left the feed to `/?launch=<id>`. On the main page Copy link and the share sheet give the launch page when one exists (`PAGES`, the ids of the inlined feed) and each row has a Launch page link; Share to Astro still sends `?launch=<id>`, which keeps working. Never put anything on these pages that is not in the feed or providers.json.
- Share cards and search: each launch page's `og:image` and `twitter:image` is its own card, `launch/<id>/share.jpg` (`share_image` in `launch_pages.py`, Pillow): 1200 by 630 JPEG, about 40 KB, deep space with the planet's lit rim, the provider's logo (PNG copies in `assets/providers/png/`, made by `node scripts/render_logo_pngs.cjs`; run it again when a logo changes), the short name (`short_name`, a port of `ORB.shortName`: **change the two together**; `tests/test_seo.py` checks they agree), the date and time in UTC, the vehicle and pad, and the Astro Radar mark; with `og:image:alt`. Without Pillow the page keeps the site's `og-image.jpg` (CI installs Pillow; the deploy needs the pip line proposed in OVERNIGHT3.md). The main page carries an ItemList of the next ten launches as Events (`LD_MAIN` in `build.py`), each pointing at its launch page and card. `tests/test_seo.py` checks every page's title, description, canonical, Open Graph and Twitter tags, structured data, image, the sitemap and robots.txt.
- Calendar (`docs/ALERTS-DESIGN.md`): `launches.ics` (every upcoming launch, stable UIDs, UTC times, alerts a day and 30 minutes before, folded lines) and `launch/<id>/launch.ics`, at build time; Subscribe to calendar shows under the Launches list and in the footer only when the build had live launches. `icsFor` in `app.js` makes the same event for the hero and rows, so **change `ics_event` and `icsFor` together**. No alert server and no stored data: email and Astro push are designs only.
- Installable and offline: `manifest.webmanifest` and the icons in `assets/` (rendered from the mark: `icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png`, `icon-maskable-512.png`). `sw.js` is versioned by a hash of the page and every file it keeps, so each deploy replaces it and its shell cache. It keeps only the main page, the photo, fonts, icons, logos, manifest and `world.js`, the last good `launches.json`, the world's textures as the page loads them (cache `radar-world-<hash of the textures>`, so a deploy that leaves them alone keeps them; the page asks for them again once the worker controls it, `swWarm`, so a first visit is kept too; offline with none saved, the world falls back to the saved photo), and the footer loop's poster and video as the page loads them (cache `radar-loop-<hash of assets/loop>`; a video asks for byte ranges, which a cache cannot hold, so the worker fetches the whole file once and answers each range from the copy as a 206, `ranged`, which Safari needs; offline with none saved, the poster or the dark band shows); the page and launches.json go to the network first. The saved launches.json comes back with `X-Astro-Offline`, and the page then says "Offline, showing last update" (`offline()` in `app.js`); never let saved data show as live. `app.js` registers it only over https or on localhost, when the page has the manifest link (not in the artifact).
- Content security policy (a meta tag the build writes, `csp_for`): scripts only from this site or the hashes of the inline scripts in the final page, data only from this site, `api.weather.gov` and the `FEED_URL` origin. A new origin the page fetches must be added to `CONNECT` in `build.py`. Launch pages have a stricter policy with no inline script.
- Space look (the blocks at the end of the style block): a deep navy page (`--bg`, `--navy`) with a cool blue accent (`--cool`, `--accent`) and a restrained warm tone (`--warm`, the horizon and a few highlights). Blocks are soft glass (`--glass` over `--base`, inner glow `--inner`, radius `--r`) with no outline; dividers are hairlines that fade at both ends (`--hair`, `--vhair`); at most one edge of a block is bright (the selected launch's left edge). Borders that set a size are kept transparent, so a restyle never moves anything. Do not bring back outlined boxes or heavy purple.
- Starfield (`#stars`, `SF` and `sfBuild`/`sfDraw`/`sfFrame` in `app.js`): one fixed canvas behind the page (the desktop hero's own layer is opaque over it; the hero loop's sky is transparent, so the stars show there), light but clearly visible: three depths of stars each drawn once into a 384 px tile and copied with an offset that drifts sideways, never with the scroll; a few stars twinkle on their own; a pre-drawn streak shoots by every 25 to 70 seconds. It draws only while some of it shows below the hero and the tab is visible, at 30 frames a second on the high tier; on phones (the lite tiers) and the low tier it is drawn once and kept still, and a few stars twinkle instead (`twinkles`, `.twk`: three groups of five, each one small element whose stars are box shadows, opacity only, run by the compositor, paused while the page scrolls; fifteen separate layers cost the phone about 2 fps in a fling); with reduced motion everything is still. `.neb` (the old nebula) is off. A tier step down rebuilds it.
- One background: the body is a single dark gradient that stays the page color (`--bg`) down to the hero's foot (`--hero-h`, set by `sizeHero`, so the hero fades into exactly the color under it), turns a slightly lighter navy (`--bg-2`) over the next screens and returns to the page color at the bottom, where the footer's planet rises out of it.
- One palette (the "one palette" block at the end of the style block): every background on the page is one of the tokens `--bg`, `--bg-2`, `--surface` (cards and rows, 90 percent), `--surface-2` (menus, inputs, chips), `--bar` (the header and the phone tab bar, opaque), `--line-c` and `--tint` (hairline and tint blues), `--accent`, `--cool`, `--cool-2`, `--cyan`, `--btn`/`--btn-2` (the solid button), the status colors (`--go`, `--amber`, `--live`, `--bad`), the text colors and `--star`, at any opacity, or white or black for highlights and shadows. `tests/palette.cjs` fails on any other background color (provider chips, logos and media aside). Add a color by adding a token, never a literal.
- One navigation bar: over 900 px the top bar (the logo, the four section links, the next launch pill) is the only bar, and its links switch the sections; the `.tabbar` is hidden there. At 900 px and under the top bar is compact (the mark and the pill) and `.tabbar` is fixed at the bottom of the screen with safe area padding (the body is padded by its height); choosing a section there scrolls to it.
- Footer planet (`#footPlanet`, `initFootPlanet` in `app.js`, `.foot-planet` in the limb block): a planet horizon rising from the bottom edge under the footer text (the footer pads by `--fp-h`, 30vw and at least 250 px, below its text, so the rim shows at every width). It is a short seamless loop rendered offline from this same world, not a second live WebGL context: `node scripts/render_loops.cjs footer` (after a build; Playwright, a Chrome with a GPU, ffmpeg; the same script renders the two hero loops, `hero-phone` and `hero-wide`) renders a low orbit limb view at sunrise frame by frame (the sun set by hand, the cloud shell drifting, the camera on a small closed path so the ground is the same at both ends), fades the frames past the loop's end into its start, and encodes `assets/loop/`: `planet-1280.mp4`/`.webm` (1280 by 720), `planet-720.mp4`/`.webm` (720 by 404; H.264 4:2:0 needs an even height) and `planet-poster.webp`, each file under 1.5 MB. The poster is a lazy image in the markup; the video is made only when the footer comes within 600 px (720 wide on phones, WebM where VP9 plays and the browser is not Safari, else MP4), muted, looped, inline, plays only while on screen and the tab is visible, and is never loaded with reduced motion, with Save Data or on 2G. If the browser refuses to play it (iOS Low Power Mode), the poster stays. A soft gradient into the page at its top and a slow glow pulse (CSS opacity) frame it. The artifact carries the poster inline and no video. Render it again if the world's look changes.
- Recent launches strip: a scroller with mandatory snap points, no scrollbar, edges that fade into the page gutter (`--fw`), and arrows on a pointer device of 768 px and up (`rcEdges`): the arrows keep their place from the first paint and turn visible only when the strip overflows, each off at its end.
- Provider logos (`assets/providers/`, sources and licenses in `assets/providers/SOURCES.md`): white, cropped SVGs from Wikimedia Commons (public domain or CC0) shown at 80 percent, full on hover, on the hero (`#heroLogo`, written into the markup by `hero_static`), each row, the Recent cards, the Watch tab provider cards and each launch page; a monogram badge (`.pmono`) when there is none. Each logo sits on a chip (`logoChip` in `app.js`, `logo_chip` in `launch_pages.py`): sized from its viewBox by the area rule (3.2 times the slot height squared) at 28 px in rows, 36 px in the hero and 30 px on cards, no wider than the slot but never under 10 px tall (`chipSize`/`chip_size`), so nothing moves when it loads; a mark or monogram carries the short name (once, when the monogram is the name). The chip's edge and glow take the provider's `brand` color from providers.json (not on the launch pages, whose policy allows no inline style). `logoChip`/`logo_chip`, `chipSize`/`chip_size`, `logo_of`/`provLogo` and `mono_of`/`monoOf` are ports of each other, so **change each pair together**; `tests/test_logos.py` checks the chip sizes agree. The build copies them to `dist/providers/`, the service worker keeps them, and the artifact carries them as data URIs. Add a logo only from a source that allows it, listed in SOURCES.md, made white with every fill and stroke `#fff`; the footer and launch pages say logos are their owners' trademarks, shown only to identify the provider.
- Space today (`scripts/fetch_nasa.py`, `renderSpace` in `app.js`, `#spaceToday` at the bottom of Launches, `nasa_photo` in `launch_pages.py`): up to four NASA cards fetched at build time into `data/nasa.json` (images in `data/nasa/`, copied to `dist/nasa/`): Earth today (EPIC, no key), the Sun (DONKI flares and storms of the last 3 days with a rough aurora outlook from the highest Kp; tried through api.nasa.gov, then its own host), the picture of the day (APOD; embedded only with no copyright notice and a real answer, else a link out with its credit) and the week's asteroids (NeoWs; distances through `distTxt`), plus a landscape photo from NASA's Image and Video Library on each NASA launch page (no key; posters, logos and graphics skipped). The key comes only from `NASA_API_KEY`, else `DEMO_KEY` (30 an hour, 50 a day per address; a run makes at most seven keyed calls); it is never written, printed or sent to the page. A source that fails keeps its last good entry marked stale, and the build marks anything over two days old stale (the card then says when it was last updated). The section is collapsed on phones and open on desktops, has credits, source links and a not affiliated line, and its details show only in Nerd mode. The artifact leaves it out. `pages.yml` does not run the fetch yet (it must not be edited here): the proposed step is in OVERNIGHT3.md; until then the committed `data/nasa.json` is what the site shows, so run `python3 scripts/fetch_nasa.py` and commit it now and then. Tests: `tests/test_nasa.py` on the answers in `tests/nasa/`.
- Weather comes from the National Weather Service in the visitor's browser. If anything live fails, the page falls back to built in sample data and labels it "Sample".

## Rules of thumb

- Brand: Astro Brand Kit colors (page #04050C, accent #B9A8FF, violet #7C5CFF, cyan #3FD0F0); the page itself now runs on the space look's navy, cool blue and warm tokens, with violet kept to the mark. The mark in `assets/astro-mark.svg` is the official file from astro-app. `build.py` generates both the nav icon (`i-astro` symbol, at the `__MARK__` marker in `src/template.html`) and the favicon from it (the favicon sits on a dark tile, so the white A shows on a light browser tab), so replace only that file. It is the mark in the nav, the footer, the favicon, the apple touch icon and the share image.
- Text stays readable over the brightest part of the world: WCAG AA (4.5 to 1, 3 to 1 for large text) against bright clouds, on a dark surface, never on the planet: the planet is a horizon below the hero copy and below the footer text, never a backdrop to text; body text at least 16 px on phones and 15 px on desktops.
- Visibility results are a modeled estimate, never a promise. Keep that wording honest in the UI: say what is assumed (the flight heading is guessed from the orbit), say when clouds are not counted, and lean cautious.
- Radar has no accounts, sign in or posts. The only things it keeps are in this browser's localStorage: the home ZIP (`radar.zip`, `radar.zipinfo`), set reminders (`radar.rem`) and Nerd mode (`radar.nerd`). Do not add a demo login or invented users, posts or counts; anything that looks live must be live.
- No hyphens in copy shown to the user.
