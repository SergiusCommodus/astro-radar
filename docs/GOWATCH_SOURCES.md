### The "jellyfish" (twilight) effect

- What it is: at twilight the ground is dark while the rocket high up is still in sunlight, so the expanding exhaust plume glows. Time Out Los Angeles (June 19, 2024, SECONDARY): a Falcon 9 from Vandenberg at 8:40 p.m., about 30 minutes after sunset, seen across Southern California including Los Angeles; launches are sometimes seen as far as Arizona; from L.A. look west or northwest as the rocket moves south.
- FOX Weather (Aug 8, 2026, SECONDARY): water vapor in the exhaust freezes into ice crystals; launches near twilight can look rainbow or iridescent; launches in full dark or daylight are much less likely to show it.
- No official (Space Force or SpaceX) explanation page was read.

## E. First launch guide facts

| Topic | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Sound delay | Sound covers about a mile every 5 seconds: NWS lightning guidance says to count the seconds between flash and thunder "and then divide by 5" for miles. So from 10 miles the roar arrives about 50 seconds after liftoff (derived, not quoted) | NWS https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-thunder (no date) |
| How far it is heard | Shuttle launches heard 35, sometimes 40, miles inland with the wind right; a Florida Tech professor estimated about 45 miles for Artemis II; wind must blow in from the Atlantic to carry it inland; trees and buildings absorb sound; the rocket "would be well into the sky by the time you hear" | ClickOrlando (Mar 24, 2026, SECONDARY) |
